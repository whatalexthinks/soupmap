Suginami ward, on Tokyo's western Chuo Line, is a residential area of small neighbourhoods: Ogikubo, Asagaya, Koenji and Nishi-Ogikubo. Ogikubo became one of Tokyo's best-known ramen towns in the 1980s and 1990s, when television and magazines made its shops famous. The style associated with it is old-fashioned Tokyo chuka soba: a clear, soy-seasoned broth of chicken or pork with niboshi (dried sardines) and other dried seafood, curly noodles, and simple toppings of chashu, menma and green onion. Some of the shops that made Ogikubo's name have served it since the postwar years.

Koenji, with its live-music bars and secondhand shops, and Asagaya add plenty of newer counters, from rich tonkotsu-gyokai to tsukemen. Suginami is a good place to see everyday Tokyo ramen away from the big stations, and the Chuo Line makes it easy to hop between neighbourhoods. ## Know before you go
- Neighbourhood shops often close one or two days a week; check before making a special trip.
- The Chuo Line stops at Koenji, Asagaya and Ogikubo, a few minutes apart.
