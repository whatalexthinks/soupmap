Chiyoda ward's Kanda district has been a soba neighbourhood since the Edo period, when buckwheat noodles became Tokyo's fast food. Tokyo's soba tradition is often described in three lineages: Yabu, known for green-tinged noodles and a strong, salty dipping sauce; Sarashina, made from the pale inner part of the buckwheat; and Sunaba. Kanda is home to some of the oldest houses still operating, including Kanda Yabu Soba, founded in 1880 and rebuilt after a fire in 2013, and other soba houses that date to the Meiji era.

Edo-style soba is usually eaten cold, as seiro or mori on a bamboo tray, dipped briefly in a dark, sharp sauce; locals dip only the tips. At the end, the shop brings soba-yu, the cloudy water the noodles were cooked in, to stir into the remaining sauce and drink. Old soba houses are also places to drink sake with small dishes in the afternoon. ## Know before you go
- Dip just the ends of the noodles: Tokyo-style sauce is strong.
- Soba-yu at the end is part of the meal; pour it into your dipping cup and drink.
