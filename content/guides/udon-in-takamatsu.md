Kagawa, Japan's smallest prefecture, calls itself the udon prefecture, and Takamatsu is its capital and main gateway. The local noodle is Sanuki udon, named after the old province: thick, square-edged wheat noodles with koshi, a firm, springy bite that comes from kneading (traditionally by foot) and resting the dough. People here eat udon for breakfast and lunch, and the city has hundreds of udon shops, from self-service counters near offices to rural noodle factories that serve bowls on the side.

Udon shops in Kagawa keep unusual hours: many open early in the morning and close in the early afternoon, or when the noodles run out. For first-timers, self-service (serufu) shops are the most local experience: you order your noodles, warm them yourself, add toppings and tempura from the counter, and pay at the end. Kagawa even has udon taxis, which drive visitors between shops. See the [Sanuki udon in Takamatsu](/guides/sanuki-udon-in-takamatsu/) list for shops named for the style, read [Sanuki udon: a whole prefecture built on a bite](/journal/sanuki-udon/), and see the neighbouring [Marugame](/guides/udon-in-marugame/) guide.

## Know before you go
- Go early: many shops close by early afternoon or when the noodles run out.
- A small bowl (sho) is normal; locals visit two or three shops in a morning.
