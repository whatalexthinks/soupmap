Shibuya ward stretches from the famous scramble crossing to Harajuku, Yoyogi and Ebisu, and its ramen is as varied as its neighbourhoods. Around Shibuya Station you'll find everything from tonkotsu chains open around the clock to small counters on the side streets of Dogenzaka and Udagawa-cho. Harajuku and Omotesando add bowls aimed at the shopping crowds, and Yoyogi and Sasazuka have quieter neighbourhood shops.

Ebisu, at the ward's southern end, is closely associated with a lighter style: shio and shoyu broths with chicken and dashi, perfumed with yuzu citrus. The best-known example is Afuri, which began in Ebisu and now has branches in Japan and abroad. Ramen in Shibuya tends to be busy at lunch and again late at night, and many places have ticket machines with English labels. For a taste of the light style at home, try our [shio ramen recipe](/recipes/shio-ramen/) with a strip of yuzu peel; for ordering help, read [Slurp away: a gentle guide to ramen-shop manners](/journal/slurping-etiquette/).

## Know before you go
- Yuzu shio is light: a good contrast if you've been eating tonkotsu.
- Weekend evenings around the crossing are crowded; side streets are calmer.
