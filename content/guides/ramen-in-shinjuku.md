Shinjuku is a ward, not just a station, and its ramen spreads well beyond the crowds of Shinjuku Station, which is often cited as the busiest railway station in the world. Around the station's east and west exits, ramen counters run from tsukemen and rich tonkotsu-gyokai to clear shoyu and the bitter-savoury niboshi (dried sardine) bowls Tokyo has embraced, many open late for the nightlife of Kabukicho and Golden Gai. Ticket machines are the norm: choose, pay, hand over the ticket, sit.

North of the station, Takadanobaba, near Waseda University, has long been one of Tokyo's ramen battlegrounds, with many shops on a short stretch competing for students and office workers. Shin-Okubo, Yotsuya and Kagurazaka add their own neighbourhood counters. With almost two hundred places on the list, the map is the easiest way to find what's near you. Read [The ticket machine: ordering ramen without saying a word](/journal/ticket-machine/) and [Read a ramen menu: 40 words that help](/journal/ramen-menu-glossary/) before your first bowl.

## Know before you go
- Most counters use ticket machines; some newer ones take card or IC payment.
- Lunch and the last-train hours are the busiest times around the station.
