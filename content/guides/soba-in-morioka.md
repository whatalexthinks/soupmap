Morioka, the capital of Iwate in northern Tohoku, is known for wanko soba, one of the most playful ways to eat noodles in Japan. Servers stand by your table with a tray of tiny portions of hot soba in broth, each about a mouthful, and flick a new one into your small lacquered bowl the moment you've finished the last, calling out to encourage you. The meal goes on until you put the lid on your bowl. Shops keep count, often with a stack of empty bowls, and some hand out certificates for passing a hundred. The custom is usually linked to the region's tradition of hospitality, serving guests until they can't eat any more.

Wanko soba is one of Morioka's three famous noodles, with jajamen (thick noodles with a miso meat sauce, influenced by Chinese zhajiangmian) and Morioka reimen (chewy cold noodles in a beef broth with kimchi). It is also listed among Japan's three great soba with Togakushi and Izumo. Read [Soba: a beginner's guide to buckwheat noodles](/journal/soba-guide/) for the basics before you start counting bowls.

## Know before you go
- Close the lid on your bowl when you're done; until then, the server keeps going.
- Most wanko soba shops recommend booking at busy times.
