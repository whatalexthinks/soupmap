Taito ward covers Asakusa, Ueno and Okachimachi, the old shitamachi (downtown) of Tokyo, and it holds an important place in ramen history. In 1910 a restaurant called Rairaiken opened in Asakusa, serving Chinese-style noodles in a soy-seasoned broth to Japanese customers. It's often described as Tokyo's first ramen shop of its kind, and its clear, brown shoyu bowl set the pattern for Tokyo chuka soba. The original closed long ago, but the style it popularised is still served all over the ward.

Today Asakusa's ramen sits among tempura houses, soba shops and the crowds visiting Senso-ji temple. Ueno and Okachimachi, around the Ameyoko market under the railway tracks, have busy, inexpensive counters for shoppers and museum-goers, and Uguisudani and Iriya add neighbourhood shops. ## Know before you go
- Asakusa gets very busy around Senso-ji at weekends: eat early or a few streets away.
- Old-style chuka soba is light; it pairs well with a plate of gyoza.
