Tokushima, on Shikoku, is known for one of Japan's most distinctive regional bowls. The broth comes in three colours. The brown style, the one the city is best known for, is a dark tonkotsu-shoyu seasoned richly with soy. The yellow style is a lighter pork-and-chicken soy broth, and the white style is a paler tonkotsu. Instead of chashu slices, the classic topping is bara niku: thin strips of pork belly simmered sweet and savoury in soy and sugar. Many shops will add a raw egg, which you break into the hot broth, and the bowl is traditionally eaten with rice, almost as a side dish.

Tokushima ramen became nationally famous from the late 1990s and is now sold in instant versions, but the shops in the city are the real thing; they cluster around Tokushima Station and the downtown streets near the Shinmachi river. The city is also famous for the Awa Odori dance festival each August. Read [Tokushima ramen: brown, yellow, white and a raw egg on top](/journal/tokushima-ramen/).

## Know before you go
- A raw egg is usually an extra; order it from the ticket machine.
- Rice is part of the way locals eat it: try a mouthful of pork belly over rice.
