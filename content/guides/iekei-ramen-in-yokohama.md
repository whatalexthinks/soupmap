Iekei (家系, house lineage) takes its name from Yoshimuraya, the shop that opened in Yokohama in 1974, and from the many shops trained in or inspired by it, whose names typically end in -ya (家, house). The bowl is a blend of Kyushu and Tokyo ideas: a rich tonkotsu (pork-bone) broth seasoned with shoyu, glossy with chicken oil, over thick, straight, chewy noodles. Toppings are fixed and recognisable: spinach, a few large sheets of nori, and chashu, often smoky.

Part of the ritual is customisation. Shops typically let you choose noodle firmness (katasa), broth strength (aji no koi-sa) and the amount of oil (abura), and many keep garlic, ginger, doubanjiang and pickles on the counter. Regulars wrap rice in the soaked nori, so a bowl of rice is a common side order. Iekei is now found across Japan, but Yokohama still has the deepest concentration, and the lineage matters to fans. ## Know before you go
- First time: order everything 'futsu' (regular), then adjust next visit.
- A small bowl of rice with the nori is part of the experience, not a substitute for it.
