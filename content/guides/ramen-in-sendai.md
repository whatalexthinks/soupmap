Sendai, the largest city in Tohoku, doesn't have one signature bowl the way Sapporo or Hakata does. Its ramen scene is broad: shoyu and miso shops, rich tonkotsu-shoyu, tsukemen and plenty of old-style chuka soba. Many of the counters gather around Sendai Station and in Kokubuncho, the city's nightlife district a short walk west.

What Sendai claims is hiyashi chuka: cold ramen noodles under strips of ham, egg and cucumber with a sweet-sour soy dressing, a summer dish across Japan. The restaurant Ryutei-ro in Sendai is often credited with serving an early version in the 1930s, though, like many food origin stories, it has rivals. If you visit in the warm months, look for a hiyashi chuka sign; in winter, look for a hot miso bowl. Sendai is also known for its gyutan (grilled beef tongue), which a few ramen shops borrow as a topping. For the bigger picture of where each regional style comes from, see [A traveller's map of Japan's regional ramen](/journal/regional-ramen-map/).

## Know before you go
- Hiyashi chuka is usually seasonal: a sign saying 冷やし中華始めました means it's back for summer.
- Kokubuncho counters stay open late; station shops suit a quick bowl between trains.
