In Osaka, udon comes before ramen. The local style is soft noodles in a pale, golden broth made from kombu and bonito, seasoned with light soy so the dashi does the talking. The city's signature is kitsune udon: noodles topped with a pocket of fried tofu (aburaage) simmered sweet, said to be a fox's favourite food, hence the name. It's usually credited to Usami-tei Matsuba-ya, an udon shop in the Senba business district that has served it since the late 1800s.

Osaka also shuffles the names. Ask for tanuki here and you'll usually get soba with fried tofu, not the tempura-scrap bowl Tokyo calls tanuki. From the city's southern suburbs comes kasu udon, topped with crisp fried beef intestine. Udon shops cluster around Umeda, Namba and the old business districts, and many also serve curry udon and seasonal bowls. ## Know before you go
- In Osaka, 'kitsune' is udon with fried tofu; 'tanuki' is usually soba with fried tofu.
- Lunch sets with a small rice bowl are common and good value.
