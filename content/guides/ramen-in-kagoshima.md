Kagoshima, at the southern tip of Kyushu, makes a pork-bone ramen, but it's often said to have developed separately from the Kurume family, with influences from Chinese cooks. The broth is cloudy but lighter than Hakata's or Kurume's, because pork bones are cooked with chicken, vegetables, and sometimes dried fish, and the noodles are usually medium-thick and straight rather than very thin. Toppings vary more than elsewhere in Kyushu: chashu, bean sprouts, green onion, and in some shops fried onion or wood-ear mushroom. Kagoshima's famous Kurobuta black pork turns up in many bowls.

The habit visitors remember is the welcome. Many Kagoshima ramen shops set out pickled daikon and a cup of tea when you sit down, to nibble while the noodles cook. Bowls cluster around Tenmonkan, the city's main arcade district, and near Kagoshima-Chuo Station; the volcano Sakurajima across the bay is never far from view. ## Know before you go
- The pickles on the counter are free; they're meant for the wait.
- Kagoshima bowls are milder than Hakata's: a good start if tonkotsu is new to you.
