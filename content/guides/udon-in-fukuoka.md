Fukuoka is famous for ramen, but locals eat a great deal of udon, and the local style is the opposite of Kagawa's firm Sanuki noodles. Hakata udon is soft and gentle, served in a light, clear dashi of kelp, bonito and small dried fish. The classic topping is gobo-ten, thin strips of burdock fried in tempura batter, which soften into the broth; maru-ten, a round fried fish cake, is another favourite. Many shops are fast, cheap and open early.

The city also claims a place in noodle history. At Jotenji temple in Hakata, a stone marks it as the birthplace of udon and soba in Japan, honouring the monk Enni (Shoichi Kokushi), who is said to have brought noodle-making techniques from Song-dynasty China in the 13th century. Historians debate the details, but the monument is a popular stop. Udon shops cluster around Hakata Station and Tenjin. Read [Japan's three great udon, and the fight for third place](/journal/three-great-udon/) for where Hakata's soft noodles fit, and see [Ramen in Fukuoka](/guides/ramen-in-fukuoka/) for the city's other bowl.

## Know before you go
- Gobo-ten is burdock tempura; it's best eaten once it has softened a little in the broth.
- Udon here is soft by design, not overcooked.
