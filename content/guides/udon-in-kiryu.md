Kiryu, in Gunma Prefecture north of Tokyo, was one of Japan's great silk-weaving towns, and its signature noodle is as distinctive as its fabrics. Himokawa udon is wheat dough rolled thin and cut into very wide, flat ribbons, often several centimetres across and sometimes much wider, so a single noodle can cover a plate. The texture is smooth and slippery, with a gentle chew, and the wide surface carries a lot of sauce. It's served cold with a dipping sauce in summer and hot in a soy-dashi broth in winter, sometimes with tempura on the side.

The town has promoted himokawa as a local speciality, and udon shops in Kiryu serve it alongside regular udon and soba. Kiryu's old weaving houses and sawtooth-roofed factories make it a pleasant day trip from Tokyo. Gunma is also known for Mizusawa udon, near Shibukawa, which sometimes appears in the popular argument over Japan's three great udon. ## Know before you go
- Eat himokawa cold first if you can: the texture is clearest that way.
- Fold a ribbon over your chopsticks; it's easier than trying to slurp it.
