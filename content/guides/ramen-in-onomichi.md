Onomichi is a hillside port town on the Seto Inland Sea, known for its temple-lined slopes, its cats and as the Honshu end of the Shimanami Kaido, the cycling route that crosses the islands to Shikoku. Its ramen is one of the best-known regional styles in western Japan. The broth is soy-seasoned, usually built on chicken bones with small dried fish or seafood from the Inland Sea, giving it a clean, savoury depth. The noodles are flat and medium in width, and the signature is a scattering of small pieces of pork back fat (seabura) on top, which melt into the soup and add sweetness.

Ramen shops in Onomichi cluster along the long shopping arcade parallel to the waterfront and near the station, and some have drawn queues for decades. After a day on the Shimanami Kaido or climbing to Senkoji temple, a bowl here is a common reward. ## Know before you go
- Queues form at lunch on weekends; mid-afternoon is quieter where shops stay open.
- The back fat is part of the bowl; some shops will go lighter if asked.
