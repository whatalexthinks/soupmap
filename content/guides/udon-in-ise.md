Ise, home to Ise Jingu, the most important shrine in Shinto, has fed pilgrims for centuries, and its udon is built for them. Ise udon noodles are very thick and boiled for a long time until they're soft and pillowy, almost the opposite of Kagawa's firm Sanuki udon. Instead of a broth, they come with a small amount of dark, glossy sauce made from tamari-style soy, mirin and dashi, topped with chopped green onion; you mix the noodles through the sauce before eating. Because the noodles are kept ready, a bowl arrives almost as soon as you order, which suited travellers on a long walk.

You'll find it in shops along the old approach streets near the Inner Shrine (Naiku), including the restored Edo-style Okage Yokocho, and near Ise-shi and Uji-Yamada stations. The style is often mentioned with Hakata udon as Japan's softest. ## Know before you go
- Mix the noodles through the sauce from the bottom before you start.
- The streets near the Inner Shrine are busiest late morning; eat early or mid-afternoon.
