The old province of Shinshu, today's Nagano Prefecture, is Japan's most famous soba region. Buckwheat grows well in its cool, high fields and thin soil, and Shinshu soba is a name you'll see on noodle packets across the country. In Nagano city, the streets leading up to Zenko-ji, one of Japan's most visited temples, are lined with soba shops serving pilgrims as they have for centuries.

Up in the mountains north-west of the city, Togakushi, a place of old shrines and cedar forests, has its own style: Togakushi soba is served in several small, coiled bundles (bocchi-mori) on a round bamboo basket, usually cold, and it often appears on lists of Japan's three great soba with Morioka's wanko soba and Izumo soba. Autumn brings shin-soba, the new harvest, when the noodles are at their most fragrant. ## Know before you go
- Look for 新そば (shin-soba) in autumn: the new crop.
- Togakushi is a bus ride from Nagano Station; combine soba with the shrines.
