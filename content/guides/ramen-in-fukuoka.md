Fukuoka is where most of Japan's tonkotsu ramen comes from, or at least where it's most at home. The local bowl is Hakata ramen: pork bones boiled hard for hours until the broth turns milky and rich, very thin straight noodles, and simple toppings of chashu, green onion and wood-ear mushroom. Because the thin noodles go soft quickly, portions are small, and you order a refill of noodles, kaedama, rather than a bigger bowl. Shops ask how firm you want them, from soft to barely cooked.

The city is also known for yatai, open-air food stalls that set up in the evening along the Nakasu riverside, in Tenjin and in Nagahama, where ramen sits beside oden, skewers and drinks at a counter shared with strangers. Nagahama, by the fish market, gave its name to a closely related style. Most of the ramen in this guide is tonkotsu, but Fukuoka has other bowls too, including a strong tantanmen and chicken scene. ## Know before you go
- Firmness, from firm to softest: barikata, kata, futsu, yawa. Kona-otoshi and harigane are barely cooked.
- Say 'kaedama' for a noodle refill before you finish your broth.
- Yatai are cash-only and seat a handful of people: be ready to squeeze in.
