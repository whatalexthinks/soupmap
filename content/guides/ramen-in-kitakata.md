Kitakata, in the Aizu region of Fukushima, has a population of under fifty thousand and a very large number of ramen shops for its size, which is why it's counted among Japan's great ramen towns alongside Sapporo and Hakata. The local bowl is clear and gentle: a soy-seasoned broth from pork bones and dried sardines (niboshi), with wide, flat, wavy noodles called hirauchi chijiremen that hold a lot of soup. Toppings are classic: slices of chashu, menma, green onion.

The habit visitors remember is asa-ra, short for asa ramen, morning ramen. Some shops open early in the morning, a custom often traced to factory and farm workers coming off night shifts. Kitakata is also a town of kura, traditional storehouses with thick plastered walls, and a walk between them fills the gap between breakfast and a second bowl. Read [Kitakata: the town that eats ramen for breakfast](/journal/kitakata-morning-ramen/), and see the [Kitakata ramen style guide](/guides/style/kitakata-ramen/) for shops elsewhere that carry the name.

## Know before you go
- Morning shops can close early in the afternoon: plan ramen first, sightseeing after.
- The bowl is light; locals often have a second one.
