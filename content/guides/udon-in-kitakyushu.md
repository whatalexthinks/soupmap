Kitakyushu, at the northern tip of Kyushu, shares the soft, gentle udon of the rest of Fukuoka Prefecture: tender noodles in a light dashi, topped with burdock tempura, fried fish cake or beef. Its own contribution is yaki udon, udon noodles stir-fried with cabbage, pork and a savoury sauce, finished with bonito flakes. The usual story is that it was first made at a small restaurant in Kokura shortly after the Second World War, when the buckwheat-and-wheat noodles used for yakisoba were scarce and the cook used dried udon instead. The city now promotes Kokura as the birthplace of yaki udon.

Udon shops are spread across Kokura, Moji, Yahata and Tobata, Kitakyushu's old districts, and many are cheap, fast and busy at lunch. The city's ramen is mostly tonkotsu, like Fukuoka's. If you're passing through Kokura Station on the Shinkansen, the area around it is the easiest place to try both. For the soft udon tradition, read [Japan's three great udon, and the fight for third place](/journal/three-great-udon/), and see [Udon in Fukuoka](/guides/udon-in-fukuoka/) for the prefecture's capital.

## Know before you go
- Yaki udon is often on izakaya and teppan menus rather than at udon shops.
- Soft noodles and a clear broth are the local norm.
