Matsumoto, in central Nagano Prefecture, sits on a high plain ringed by the Japan Alps, and like much of Shinshu it's a soba town. Buckwheat thrives in the region's cool climate and big day-night temperature swings, and the city's soba shops serve noodles made from local flour, most often cold on a bamboo tray with a dipping sauce, so the buckwheat's flavour comes through. Many shops make their noodles by hand on the premises, and some offer juwari, 100% buckwheat, alongside the more common blend with a little wheat.

The shops cluster near Matsumoto Castle, one of Japan's few surviving original castle keeps and a National Treasure, and along the old merchant street of Nakamachi with its white storehouses. Autumn is the season for new soba, and the city celebrates the harvest with soba events. ## Know before you go
- Juwari (100% buckwheat) noodles are more fragile and nutty; nihachi (80/20) are smoother.
- Many soba shops close once the day's noodles are gone.
