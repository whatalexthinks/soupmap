Fujiyoshida, on the northern slopes of Mount Fuji, is known for Yoshida no udon, probably the hardest, chewiest udon you'll eat in Japan. The thick noodles are kneaded firmly and cooked for a relatively short time, so they need real chewing. They're served in a soy-and-miso broth with boiled cabbage and often sakura-niku, horse meat simmered sweet, or fried tofu. On every table sits suridane, a red paste of chilli, sesame and spices that you stir in to taste.

Many udon shops in the town open only for lunch, and some are in converted homes, with no-frills seating and handwritten menus. The story often told is that udon was a quick, filling lunch in the town's textile-weaving days. Fujiyoshida is a base for the Fuji Five Lakes and for climbing Fuji, and the Chureito Pagoda viewpoint is nearby, so a bowl fits easily into a day of sightseeing. ## Know before you go
- Most shops open at lunch only and close when sold out.
- Suridane is spicy: start with a little.
