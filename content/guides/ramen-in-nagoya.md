Nagoya has a strong local food culture (locals call it Nagoya meshi), and its ramen follows suit. The city's own invention is Taiwan ramen: a small bowl of soy broth topped with minced pork stir-fried with a lot of chilli and garlic, plus garlic chives. Despite the name, it's from Nagoya. It's usually credited to the restaurant Misen, run by a Taiwanese-born family, which created it in the 1970s from a Taiwanese noodle dish, made much hotter for local tastes. Its spin-off, Taiwan mazesoba, a brothless bowl of thick noodles with the same spicy pork, raw egg yolk and garlic, also began in Nagoya, in the late 2000s.

Nagoya is also home to Sugakiya, a postwar chain known for a light pork-and-seafood ramen and a spoon with fork tines. Beyond those, the city has every style, from tonkotsu to miso, spread around Nagoya Station, Sakae and Osu. For the other noodles the city is known for, see [Udon in Nagoya](/guides/udon-in-nagoya/) and [Kishimen in Nagoya](/guides/kishimen-in-nagoya/). ## Know before you go
- Taiwan ramen is hot: shops often offer milder versions (sometimes called 'America' for mild).
- Taiwan mazesoba usually comes with a little rice at the end to mop up the sauce.
