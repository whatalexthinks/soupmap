Asahikawa sits in a basin in central Hokkaido, and its winters are among the coldest of any Japanese city. Its ramen answers that. The local style is shoyu, seasoned with soy sauce, over what cooks call a double soup: a pork or chicken stock blended with a seafood stock made from dried fish and kombu. A layer of lard floats on top, sealing in the heat so the last mouthful is nearly as hot as the first. The noodles are thin to medium, wavy and low in water, so they stay firm and soak up less soup.

The style became nationally known through the 1990s, and local shops gathered together in the Asahikawa Ramen Village, a cluster of counters in the north of the city. Asahikawa is also a common stop on the way to Asahiyama Zoo and the Daisetsuzan mountains, so many visitors eat their first bowl near the station. ## Know before you go
- The lard layer hides the heat: sip carefully.
- Shio and miso exist here too, but shoyu is the city's own.
