Himeji, the castle city west of Kobe, has a small local twist on oden that has become a point of civic pride. Himeji oden is the familiar pot of daikon, eggs, konnyaku, fried tofu and fish cakes simmered in dashi, but it's eaten with shoga-joyu, soy sauce mixed with grated ginger. Some shops pour it over the pieces, some serve it as a dipping sauce, and a few simmer the pot with it. The ginger brightens rich pieces like beef tendon and cuts through the sweetness of fish cakes. The custom is said to have started with people who simply liked their oden that way, and the city has promoted it since the 2000s.

You'll find it in izakaya, standing bars and some cafes near Himeji Station and along the shopping arcades leading towards Himeji Castle. It's a cold-weather dish, so look for it from autumn onward. Read [Oden: the winter pot that never seems to empty](/journal/oden/) for the national picture, or simmer a pot at home with our [oden recipe](/recipes/oden/) and add a ginger-soy dip.

## Know before you go
- Look for 姫路おでん or 生姜醤油 on menus.
- Try one piece with the ginger soy and one with mustard to see the difference.
