Kurume, inland from Fukuoka city, is often cited as the place where tonkotsu ramen began. The story usually told is that a street stall here served a clear pork-bone soup in the late 1930s, and that the milky broth came about after the war, when a cook let the pot boil too hard and found customers liked the cloudy result. Whatever the exact history, Kurume ramen is the root of the Kyushu family: Hakata's and Kumamoto's styles are generally described as its descendants.

Compared with Hakata ramen, Kurume's bowl is often thicker and stronger, with a deeper pork flavour, and some shops keep an old practice of topping up a long-running pot with fresh bones and broth instead of starting from scratch each day, so the soup develops over time. Noodles are thin to medium, toppings simple: chashu, green onion, nori, sometimes crisp fried pork fat. Shops are spread around the city, near JR Kurume and Nishitetsu Kurume stations.
