Wakayama's ramen became nationally famous in the late 1990s, after a local shop appeared on a television ramen contest, but it had been the city's everyday food for decades before that. Locals call it chuka soba. There are two broad families. One, often called shako-mae after an old tram depot that a famous shop stood in front of, is a clearer soy broth. The other, the style that made the city famous, is tonkotsu-shoyu: pork bones boiled hard and seasoned with soy, rich and brown, with thin straight noodles.

What visitors remember is the counter. Wakayama shops traditionally set out hayazushi, small packets of pressed mackerel sushi, and boiled eggs; you help yourself while you wait or with your bowl, and tell the staff how many you had when you pay. The city sits at the mouth of the Kinokawa river, an hour or so from Osaka, and makes an easy day trip. ## Know before you go
- Help yourself to hayazushi and eggs, and remember how many: you pay for them at the end.
- Locals often eat the sushi while waiting for the noodles.
