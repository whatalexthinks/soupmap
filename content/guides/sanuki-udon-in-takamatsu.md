Sanuki udon's character is in the bite: thick, square-cut noodles with koshi, a firmness and spring that Kagawa cooks get from long kneading and resting. Most shops offer the same handful of ways to eat them. Kake is noodles in hot, light dashi made with small dried sardines (iriko). Bukkake comes with a smaller amount of stronger sauce poured over. Kamaage is served straight from the cooking pot in its hot water, with a dipping sauce. Kamatama tosses hot noodles with a raw egg and a splash of soy, Sanuki's answer to carbonara. Zaru is chilled noodles on a bamboo tray with a dipping sauce.

At a self-service (serufu) shop the routine goes like this. Order the size and style at the counter and take your noodles. Warm them yourself in a basket (tebo) dipped in the boiling water if you want them hot, then add broth from the tap or urn. Help yourself to tempura, green onion and ginger, and pay at the end. ## Know before you go
- Watch the person in front of you at a self-service shop; the order of steps varies.
- Tempura is priced per piece and paid at the till with the noodles.
