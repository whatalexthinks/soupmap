Tsubame and its neighbour Sanjo, in central Niigata, are Japan's metalworking towns: Tsubame for Western cutlery, Sanjo for knives and tools. Their ramen grew up alongside the factories. The local style, often called Tsubame-Sanjo se-abura ramen, has a dark soy broth flavoured with niboshi (dried sardines), covered with a thick layer of chopped pork back fat (se-abura), with very thick noodles and chopped raw onion on top. The usual story is that it was made for workers who ordered delivery: the fat kept the soup hot on the way and the thick noodles didn't go soft before they arrived.

It is a heavy bowl, and shops often let you choose how much back fat you want, from a little to a lot. The style has spread beyond Niigata, but the concentration of shops here is what makes Tsubame a pilgrimage for fans. Tsubame-Sanjo Station on the Joetsu Shinkansen sits between the two towns. Compare it with [Onomichi ramen](/journal/onomichi-ramen/), another soy-and-small-fish bowl with scattered pork fat, and see the [Niigata](/guides/ramen-in-niigata/) guide for the prefecture's other styles.

## Know before you go
- Ordering less fat is normal: ask for 'abura sukuname' if the ticket machine doesn't offer it.
- The noodles are thick and take a while to cook: expect a short wait.
