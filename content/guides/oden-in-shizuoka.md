Shizuoka has one of Japan's most distinctive oden styles. The broth is dark, made with beef sinew and soy, and topped up rather than replaced, so it deepens over time. Everything is served on skewers, including kuro hanpen, a grey-black fish cake made from whole sardines or mackerel that's Shizuoka's signature piece. Before eating, you sprinkle each skewer with dashi-ko, powdered dried fish, and aonori, green seaweed flakes. The pieces are priced individually, and you count your skewers at the end.

The city's oden culture is everywhere: in izakaya, at standing counters, and traditionally at dagashi-ya, old-fashioned candy shops where children bought skewers after school. Downtown, the Aoba Oden-gai and Aoba Yokocho alleys near the Aoba Symbol Road are lined with small oden bars. Shizuoka oden is often eaten year-round, not just in winter. Read [Oden: the winter pot that never seems to empty](/journal/oden/) for how regional styles differ, or simmer a pot at home with our [oden recipe](/recipes/oden/).

## Know before you go
- Shake on fish powder and aonori from the shaker on the counter: that's the Shizuoka finish.
- Keep your skewers: they're how the bill is counted.
