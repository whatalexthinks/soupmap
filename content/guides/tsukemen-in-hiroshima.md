Most tsukemen in Japan follows the Tokyo pattern: thick noodles beside a hot, dense pork-and-fish dip. Hiroshima's version is its own thing. The noodles are thin to medium and served cold, piled with boiled cabbage, sliced cucumber and green onion, and sometimes chashu or a boiled egg. The dip is cold too: a sharp, savoury sauce of soy and vinegar with a lot of chilli oil and ground sesame. You choose the heat, usually on a numbered scale, and the top levels are very hot. The result is a refreshing, spicy bowl, especially welcome in Hiroshima's humid summers, though the shops serve it all year.

The style is usually traced to a Hiroshima restaurant in the postwar decades and is now served all over the city. Shops are concentrated downtown, around Hatchobori and Nagarekawa, and near Hiroshima Station. ## Know before you go
- Heat levels vary between shops: a '3' at one can be a '5' at another.
- Some shops offer a larger noodle portion at the same price: ask before ordering.
