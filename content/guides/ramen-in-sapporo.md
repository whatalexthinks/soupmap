Ramen arrived in Sapporo with the rest of Japan's postwar noodle boom, but the city gave the country something new in the 1950s: broth seasoned with miso. That bowl became what the rest of Japan calls Sapporo ramen, and it still sets the tone here. Minced pork, bean sprouts, cabbage and onion are seared in a very hot wok, the stock goes into the same wok to pick up the browned flavour, and only then does it meet the noodles. Those noodles are thick, curly and yellow, made to stand up to a heavy soup, and a layer of lard on top holds the heat in a cold room.

Miso is the signature, not the only option: most shops also serve shio and shoyu, and many offer butter and sweetcorn as add-ons. Bowls cluster around Susukino, the entertainment district south of Odori Park, where ramen counters stay open late, and around Sapporo Station. Read the story of the style in [Sapporo miso: the bowl built for snow](/journal/sapporo-miso-ramen/), or [make it at home](/recipes/miso-ramen/).

## Know before you go
- Many places sell tickets from a machine at the door: buy first, then sit.
- Butter and corn are usually extra toppings, not part of the standard bowl.
- For the miso-only list, see [Miso ramen in Sapporo](/guides/miso-ramen-in-sapporo/).
