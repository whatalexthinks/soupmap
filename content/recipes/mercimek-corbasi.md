*Mercimek çorbası* simply means "lentil soup", and in Turkey it's the one everybody knows. It's on the menu at almost every *lokanta* (a casual restaurant serving home-style cooking), it's made at home on weeknights, and it's eaten at any hour, breakfast included. It's cheap and quick, and far better than its short ingredient list suggests.

Red lentils do most of the work. They collapse as they cook, and with a carrot for sweetness and a potato for body they blend into a soup that's smooth and creamy without any cream. Tomato paste and Turkish red pepper paste give it colour and a gentle savoury depth, and cumin keeps it from tasting flat.

The finish is what people remember: butter heated until it foams, then swirled with *pul biber* (Turkish red pepper flakes, fruity and mildly hot) and dried mint until it turns brick red, and spooned over each bowl. Add a good squeeze of lemon at the table. The usual mistake is underseasoning. Lentil soup needs more salt and more acid than you'd expect, so taste it again once the lemon is in.

## Ingredients

### Soup
- 2 tbsp olive or sunflower oil
- 1 onion, chopped
- 1 carrot (about 100 g), peeled and chopped
- 1 small floury potato (about 150 g), peeled and diced
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tbsp Turkish red pepper paste (*biber salçası*), mild or hot
- 200 g red lentils, rinsed until the water runs clear
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1.25 l hot water, plus more to loosen
- 1 1/2 tsp salt, plus more to taste
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

### Sizzling butter
- 30 g butter
- 1 tsp pul biber (Turkish red pepper flakes)
- 1 tsp dried mint

### To serve
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Method

### Cook the soup
1. Heat the oil in a large saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook, stirring, for 6–8 minutes, until soft, translucent and just colouring at the edges. Add the carrot and potato and cook for 3 minutes more.
2. Stir in the tomato paste and pepper paste and cook for 1–2 minutes, until they darken slightly and smell sweet rather than raw.
3. Add the lentils, cumin, hot water and salt and bring to a boil. Skim off the foam, then lower the heat and simmer, partly covered, for 25–30 minutes, stirring now and then so nothing catches, until the lentils have fallen apart and a piece of potato crushes against the side of the pan with no resistance.

### Blend and season
4. Blend until completely smooth with a stick blender, or in batches in a jug blender (fill it no more than half full and hold the lid down with a folded tea towel, as hot soup expands). For an extra-fine texture, push it through a sieve afterwards.
5. Return the soup to the pan over low heat. Loosen with hot water if needed: it should pour easily from the ladle, about as thick as single (light) cream. Add the black pepper, then taste and add more salt until the lentil flavour comes through clearly. Keep it warm while you make the butter.

### Finish
6. Melt the butter in a small pan over medium heat until it foams and smells nutty, about 2 minutes. Take the pan off the heat, add the pul biber and mint and swirl for 10–15 seconds, until the butter turns brick red. Don't put it back on the heat: the pepper burns in seconds and turns bitter.
7. Ladle the soup into bowls, spoon the red butter over the top and serve with lemon wedges for squeezing.

## Tips
- Rinse the lentils well. It washes off the surface starch and dust that make the pot foam up and boil over, and gives a cleaner-tasting soup.
- Cook until everything has truly collapsed. Undercooked lentils and potato blend grainy, not silky; when a lentil has lost all its shape, you're there.
- No biber salçası? Use another 1/2 tbsp tomato paste and 1 tsp sweet paprika, with a pinch of chilli flakes if you like heat. For pul biber, Aleppo-style pepper is very close; otherwise mix 3/4 tsp sweet paprika with 1/4 tsp chilli flakes, and add it to the butter for no more than a few seconds.
- The soup thickens as it stands, even in the 10 minutes before serving. Keep a kettle of hot water nearby and adjust right before you ladle it out.
- Season with the lemon in mind. Squeeze a little into a spoonful and taste before you decide it needs more salt.

## Make ahead and storage
- Cool the soup quickly and refrigerate within 2 hours. It keeps for 4 days in the fridge or 3 months in the freezer, and sets thick when cold, so loosen it with water as you reheat it to a simmer.
- Make the butter fresh, just before serving: it's the part that doesn't keep.

## Variations
- **Oil instead of butter (vegan):** warm 3 tbsp olive oil in the small pan until it shimmers, then take it off the heat and add the pul biber and mint for 10 seconds. With water rather than stock, the whole soup is then vegan and dairy-free.
- **With stock:** use chicken stock instead of water for a rounder soup (it's then no longer vegetarian).
- **Extra-velvety:** many Turkish cooks stir 1 tbsp plain flour into the onion with the pastes in step 2. The soup is then no longer gluten-free.
